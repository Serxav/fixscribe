One method, any object. Phones, laptops, furniture, bikes, appliances, cars, plumbing and tools — and it refuses the jobs that need a professional.

Most instructions are written by someone who already knows how. They skip the step that seems obvious — the one where you get stuck. The person who just did the job knows it best, so FixScribe keeps their hands, their words and their order of work.

What FixScribe does

From one video to a guide you can trust — and help while you work.

Everything starts from a video of the job. A photo does not make a guide: it checks your own work against one.

  1. Available

    Film it once

    Record on your phone or upload a video you already have — a repair, an assembly, taking something apart. Talk through it or stay silent.

    MP4 or MOV · up to 10 minutes · any object

  2. Available

    It recognizes the job

    It works out what the object is, what you are doing to it and what can hurt you, and transcribes what you say, to the second. Gas, mains panels, airbags and refrigerant are refused before anything else runs.

    Muse Spark · Muse Voice · safety rules

  3. Available

    A guide that proves every step

    Each step names its parts, shows where they are on the frame and the screws numbered, and keeps the second of video and your own words it rests on. What cannot be proven is marked, not hidden.

    Muse Spark points · SAM 3.1 cuts out · checks in code

  4. Available

    Took it apart? It writes the way back

    From a video of taking something apart, FixScribe writes the guide to put it back together in reverse order — and checks that every screw that came out goes back in.

    Reverse guide · fastener count

  5. Available

    Check my step

    Take a photo of your own object at any step. SAM 3.1 compares it with the guide and tells you it is done — or which screws are still in place.

    One photo · SAM 3.1

  6. Available

    A coach that watches with you

    Point the camera at your work. FixScribe tells you which step you are on, whether it looks right and what to do next — out loud, hands free.

    Live camera · Muse Spark

  7. Available

    The instruction film

    A narrated film of the guide with captions and step markers: forward to take it apart, backward to put it back. The tracked parts stay lit as they move.

    SAM 3.1 tracking · voice in your language

  8. Coming next

    Replace a part

    Pick the part you need to change. FixScribe plans the shortest way to reach it, the swap, and the way back — so a spare part is one guide away.

    Parts catalog A, B, C… from the agent

Also in every guide: a printable screw tray, Markdown export, repair mode one step at a time, and the agent console to watch how the guide was proven.

How it works

Six steps, each one done by the right model.

The agent

You can watch it work.

Each turn shows what the agent decided, the tool it used and what came back. Refusals are shown too: that is the system doing its job. When it is not sure, it stops and asks you one question on the exact frame.

agent · turns 07–09
07  plan    The bolt is clearest at 12.4 s, before the hand covers it.
    act     look(t_s=12.4)
    observe frame frames/t12.4.jpg
 
08  plan    Point at the long bolt, not the short one beside it.
    act     point(frame, "long metal bolt from the bag")
    observe 1 box [412, 518, 470, 611]
 
09  act     add_part(A, "metal bolt", x4, box)
    observe refused: the cut-out fills 6% of the box

Every model has one job.

All the AI inside FixScribe is Meta's, called through the Meta Model API. Code checks what the models say — no model grades its own work.
